This is an experimental 26-port resistance-network fingerprint fixture, not an in-circuit precision ohmmeter. It is intended to flag changes such as a nominal 100 kΩ path replaced by 1 MΩ, or an open-compatible high-resistance joint. The requested 10–20% target remains a calibration goal, not a measured accuracy specification. No physical fixture has been built or measured for this release.

The PCB outline is exactly 100 × 70 mm. Minimum track width is 0.3 mm and minimum clearance is 0.3 mm, enforced by both the project rules and Default netclass; ground pour minimum thickness is also 0.3 mm. There are no DRC exclusions or ignored checks. All routed copper is B.Cu, with no vias or wire links. KiCad necessarily represents plated through-hole pads on both copper layers, but the fabrication ZIP intentionally contains no F.Cu Gerber. For a home-etched board, drill the through-holes and solder the four headers from the copper side. Commercial fabrication must be explicitly ordered as a single-sided bottom-copper board, not an ordinary two-layer board inferred from the KiCad layer list.

J1 and J2 are 1×20 female sockets for a standard original RP2040 Raspberry Pi Pico with male header pins fitted. They are 17.78 mm apart and the Pico is rotated 180°, so its USB end faces the board edge marked PICO USB. J1 pin 1 is Pico physical pin 1, nearest that USB edge; J2 pin 1 is Pico physical pin 21, at the opposite end, so its numbering runs opposite J1. Viewed from the BOTTOM (copper) side the two 1×13 female DUT sockets sit side by side at the left edge, forming a 2×13 block with 2.54 mm pitch, and both Pico sockets are on the right. J3 is the outer (left-most in bottom view) DUT column, J4 the inner column; pin 1 of both is at the top edge in bottom view and is the rectangular pad. All four headers use oval 1.7 × 1.4 mm copper pads with 1 mm drills. This provides a 0.2 mm annular ring and space for routing and ground between the socket pads. J3 is not a direct mechanical mate for the DC3 straddle connector; use labelled flying leads or a passive harness. The mapping is enforced by the independent netmap audit, including mechanical orientation.

R1–R26 are 4.7 kΩ, 1%, 1/8 W 0805 surface-mount resistors, fitted exclusively on B.Cu. Only the four female headers use through-hole pads. C1–C26 are 100 nF 0805 ceramic capacitors, fitted exclusively on the bottom. Use one consistent dielectric/batch if possible and calibrate their actual behavior. No external IC, transistor, diode, mux, buffer, or expander is fitted. The Pico is the only active module. Pico power, ADC reference, enable and RUN contacts are deliberately not connected to DUT sockets.

Every test port connects to its own GPIO through a finite 4.7 kΩ 0805 resistor and has a 100 nF 0805 capacitor to tester USB ground. All 26 channels are routed as planar lanes on B.Cu with no vias or jumpers; ground is a single B.Cu copper pour with a 0.3 mm minimum fill thickness. The refilled 0.3 mm-clearance pour connects the GP16/17 corridor without the former redundant U-shaped GND escape. Ground islands are removed, and no arbitrary GND track/stub remains. No DUT node is hard-clamped to ground. The sense GPIO is an input, and its series resistor remains present.

Before every directed measurement, all 26 GPIOs are driven low through their series resistors for 100 ms to reduce history dependence, then charged high for 50 ms. Hidden DUT rail capacitors may require a longer discharge hold; verify golden-board stability versus hold duration. This is not a guarantee that phantom-powered internal rails are discharged. All become inputs with pulls explicitly disabled. PIO then enables one GPIO as a low output through its 4.7 kΩ resistor while timing another GPIO's first falling digital threshold crossing. All 24 other ports remain high-impedance but their capacitors stay in circuit. Each unordered pair is measured in both sink directions, for 325 pairs and 650 directed measurements per repeat.

This implementation uses full 3.3 V precharge, not low-voltage excitation. Passive attenuation would put the signal below the Pico digital input threshold; no low-voltage accuracy claim is made. Populated unpowered op-amp boards can have nonlinear protection/leakage paths at this excitation, so a known-good populated board measured with the same fixture is the authoritative baseline. The resistor-only netlist prediction is a topology aid, not a complete semiconductor model. Keep this limitation in mind once, rather than interpreting the output as individual resistor values.

The discharge network equations are

    C dv/dt + (G_DUT + e_b e_bᵀ / R_s) v = 0

where every timing capacitor contributes to C, every DUT resistor contributes to the full nodal conductance matrix, and R_s is the finite sink series resistor. During precharge, all 26 source conductances are present and the forcing vector is 3.3 × sum(e_k / R_s). The model ASSUMES an initially discharged network, integrates 50 ms of precharge, then evaluates the coupled discharge. Its no-sink mode removes the sink conductance entirely. The model does not simulate GPIO leakage, X7R absorption, threshold variation or phantom-powered ICs; real reference captures are mandatory. Full-network zero initial charge is NOT established by the 100 ms fixture reset for the extracted DC3, whose floating/capacitor-only rail modes are explicitly unqualified in evidence/reset-bound.json. DUT capacitors are stamped between their actual board nets. The net called DUT GND is floating unless explicitly wired; it is not silently tied to USB ground.

The supplied model uses scipy.linalg.eigh(G,C) to evaluate the multiexponential response and searches for the first calibrated threshold crossing. A configurable 1 pF per internal node regularizes otherwise algebraic/floating nodes. This is a modeling parameter, not a claim about the board's measured parasitics. Gmin is 1e-14 S. Never use R = t/C on this circuit. Even a directly shorted pair discharges two timing capacitors through the sink resistor; a nominal 100 Ω pair already has roughly a 0.88 ms timing floor in the ideal model. Opens are represented as timeouts, not a fabricated resistance.

PIO runs at 2 MHz and its two-instruction sampling loop gives 1 µs count steps. The sink is enabled inside the PIO program, so USB interrupts and Python scheduling do not set the measured start time. Fixed pad synchronizer/threshold/startup offsets, GPIO leakage, charge-to-start dead time, ceramic absorption, and residual DUT charge must be characterized in calibration. RP2040 Table 625 specifies up to 1 µA input leakage, so a guaranteed 30 MΩ absolute measurement is not supported; the open/reference calibration determines which high-resistance faults are distinguishable on this particular fixture. The host tests assemble the real program with the upstream MicroPython assembler and instruction-step its short, finite and timeout cases. These are software tests, not hardware timing measurements.

Peak initial current is bounded by 3.3/4700 = 0.702 mA per GPIO. A deliberately pessimistic simultaneous 26-port load is 18.26 mA total, below the RP2040 50 mA combined GPIO limit. A single resistor's maximum dissipation at 3.3 V is 2.32 mW. The 26 tester capacitors store 14.157 µJ together at 3.3 V. These bounds assume an unpowered/discharged DUT and do not make this fixture safe for externally powered circuits. The Pico pins are not 5 V tolerant.

Before attaching a DUT, unplug every DUT supply, USB cable, programmer, and connection to the motherboard. Discharge its rails with an appropriate resistor, then confirm with a meter that all test nodes and rails are near 0 V. Do not hot-plug a charged board. There is no protection against powered ±15 V daughtercards. Do not connect a scope ground clip or another grounded instrument to a floating DUT node without accounting for the new connection. Firmware does not start a scan automatically on USB power-up.

Ports 1–13 are J3 pins 1–13, using GP28, GP26, GP21, GP19, GP17, GP15, GP13, GP11, GP9, GP7, GP5, GP3, GP1. Ports 14–26 are J4 pins 1–13, using GP27, GP22, GP20, GP18, GP16, GP14, GP12, GP10, GP8, GP6, GP4, GP2, GP0. Every usable Pico GPIO is connected. GP26/27/28 are explicitly configured as digital SIO inputs/outputs using machine.Pin, just like all other ports; no ADC object or analog conversion is used. GP20/21/22 are Pico physical pins 26/27/29; GP26/27/28 are physical pins 31/32/34. manifest.json has every port, socket, GPIO and physical pin.

The example DC3 harness maps ports 1–10 to SCORE_0–SCORE_9 and ports 11–26 to ACT_0–ACT_15. The DC3 rails are not contacted. The source is boards/dc3/dc3_universal.kicad_pcb, whose SHA256 and exact pad-to-net assignments are captured in dc3-passive.json. Its actual connector is J_STRADDLE_EDGE, with score nets on B1–B10 and the first ten activation nets on A1–A10. Do not assume the board has a 20-pin matching header. Additional harness selections can be made by writing a different 26-entry port_nets JSON file and regenerating the predictions.

extract_dut.py must run in KiCad 10's Python environment. It reads actual pcbnew pad nets, parses populated resistor values and capacitors, and retains every unsupported/DNP device explicitly in omitted_devices. The extraction contains 153 resistors and six 100 nF capacitors. The three TL074 ICs are not silently replaced by ideal resistors. If an ohm-app netlist has updated measured resistor values, replace the corresponding resistor edge values while preserving actual net names and regenerate the predictions.

To predict 26 reference plus 650 directed fingerprints on a machine with NumPy and SciPy installed, run

    python3 model.py dc3-passive.json dc3-ports.json predicted.csv

The nominal falling threshold is 1.3 V only as an initial modeling parameter; set --threshold to the calibrated value. The shipped evidence/dc3-predicted.csv is labeled model output, not a real USB measurement. For a fault hypothesis, copy the extracted JSON and replace one resistor's value with 30000000 for a resistive open, remove that edge for an ideal open, or substitute 1000000 versus 100000. Generate each candidate CSV and compare its directed timing signature against measured changes. Rank candidates by normalized timing residual over all valid ports, penalizing timeout/status mismatches. Parallel paths mean several faults may remain indistinguishable; report candidate nets/components rather than falsely identifying an individual resistor.

Use an isolated calibration fixture before any populated-board work. With no DUT attached, capture at least five scans and retain all timeout/leakage patterns. Bridge each pair in turn with measured 100 Ω, 1 kΩ, 10 kΩ, 100 kΩ and 1 MΩ resistors, then with a short and an open. Run both directions, and use --timeout 20 in the model plus timeout_us=20000000 in firmware when the upper range needs it. Repeat a representative subset at operating temperature after warm-up. Clean flux and avoid touching high-resistance nodes.

For each sense GPIO, fit the effective falling threshold and offset using the full two-capacitor model and the measured reference resistors, not t/C. Fit capacitance/series-resistance variation per port when enough independent calibration data are available. Store the open/short distributions, repeated-capture median and spread, and flag a range as unquantified if reference bands overlap. No numeric high-resistance result is inferred from a timeout; an actual open is the reference condition. Detection of 100 kΩ versus a high-resistance failure still uses the whole calibrated signature.

Then record at least five captures of a known-good discharged daughtercard with the exact same harness. Keep temperature, precharge timing and firmware version fixed. Compare another board or a before/after repair capture using compare.py. It compares medians with the larger of a 20% band, six golden median absolute deviations, and a 5 µs timing floor, and preserves timeout/precharge-failure states. Matching timeouts are explicitly unquantified, never proof of matching resistance. Review multiple changed pair directions before deciding which solder joint to inspect.

Copy firmware/main.py to the Pico as main.py. From its USB MicroPython REPL, run

    import main
    main.scan(repeats=8, timeout_us=5000000)

Or, with mpremote already installed and the file copied, capture raw CSV using

    mpremote exec "import main; main.scan(repeats=8, timeout_us=5000000)" > measured.csv

Filter any REPL banners before passing the CSV to compare.py. Each row includes pair_a, pair_b, sense, sink, repeat, fall_us, status, window_us. scan() first emits all 26 no-sink self-discharge references, eight times each, followed by 325 unordered pairs in both directions, eight times each: 5,408 rows total. sink=0 is a reference sentinel, not GPIO0; references use the same discharge, precharge and timeout as pair measurements. A separate PIO program never enables an output in reference mode. Its clock starts after release/reconfiguration, so the small release-to-start dead time remains a calibration limitation. All pulls are disabled. references=False explicitly opts out; such captures cannot qualify leakage-sensitive comparisons. It may take tens of minutes when most ports time out. Ctrl-C always stops the state machine and releases every test GPIO to input.

The 100 Ω reference is a calibration check, not a supported single-shot resolution claim: its timing differs only slightly from a short because both timing capacitors discharge through the series resistor. Use at least eight repeats and per-pin threshold calibration; treat roughly 1 kΩ as the provisional single-shot lower range. Treat roughly 1 MΩ as an unvalidated practical upper range, not a guaranteed specification. No numeric 30 MΩ measurement or 30 MΩ calibration point is required. High-resistance/open-like classification is deliberately unquantified.

The available X7R capacitors remain fitted as requested. Their bias dependence and dielectric absorption can limit repeatability; reject inconsistent calibration rather than assuming nominal capacitance. C0G/NP0 or suitable film would improve this if available, but requires a footprint and assembly review. The default scan now includes no-sink self-discharge captures for every port. Hardware megohm discrimination still requires repeatable measured reference separation; implementing references alone is not a physical accuracy result.

The reset bound is reproducible with python3 reset_bound.py. For the stated 1% series resistors and 10% capacitor tolerance, Rmax=4747 ohms and Cmax=110 nF. Each of the 26 fixture caps has its OWN low-GPIO shunt, so the worst linear fixture time constant is Rmax*Cmax=0.52217 ms, not 26 times this. Passive inter-port resistors, including a chain of 25 links of 1 kohm joining all 26 ports, cannot slow the maximum-voltage envelope: at the node with highest voltage their current is outward or zero. For initial voltages between 0 and 3.3 V and ideal low GPIOs, the 100 ms hold bounds the fixture-only residual above the low level by 3.3*exp(-100/0.52217)=2.22548e-83 V. That mathematical figure is NOT a physical residual precision claim; GPIO VOL, ground offsets, dielectric absorption and unknown DUT storage dominate long before it. The independent 26-node generalized eigenvalue check reproduces the same slowest time constant.

Added DUT capacitors or nodes without their own discharge path invalidate that fixture-only bound. The extracted DC3 has floating/capacitor-only modes when artificial gmin is removed; no finite full-network reset guarantee follows from the available passive model. Firmware accepts discharge_ms=100 or any larger integer, applied identically before each reference and pair. Use an externally discharged unpowered board and establish stable golden signatures when increasing the hold, for example 100, 250 and 1000 ms. Do not describe an arbitrary hidden capacitor network as guaranteed reset. Finite leakage references do not correct history dependence.

compare.py now emits raw golden comparisons plus separate golden_quality and measured_quality guards. A pair is sink_resolved only when every repeat is valid and its slowest crossing is more than two times faster than the earliest no-sink reference crossing, or more than two times faster than the full timeout window when all reference repeats time out. Otherwise it is leakage_limited or unqualified. Matching pair/reference timeouts are open_compatible_unquantified, never proof of an actual open. No ohms are returned. Missing, mixed or failed reference captures cannot qualify a pair; the CSV carries window_us to prevent assuming a longer no-fall observation than was acquired. The guard is deliberately conservative, not a leakage-subtraction formula or guarantee of identifying a unique resistor.

evidence/reference-classification-model.json exercises an ideal isolated 100 kohm bridge (10.231852 ms), 1 Mohm bridge (94.065534 ms) and open (timeout) with five-second no-sink timeouts. Both finite bridges are sink_resolved in this ideal model and separated by more than eightfold. Tests also reject a synthetic leakage reference falling at 120 ms when a pair falls at 100 ms. These are software/model discrimination cases, NOT measurements of built hardware; accept actual 100 kohm versus 1 Mohm/open discrimination only after real fixture captures pass these reference guards and their golden bands are distinct.

TOP-side assembly and independent orientation audit
=================================================
Mount the Pico and ALL FOUR female sockets on the top/non-copper side; solder their through-hole tails on the B.Cu side. Do not mount or flip the Pico underneath. The 52 passives remain on the bottom copper side.

The F.SilkS 21×51 mm outline is the Pico PCB projection, not an installed Pico 3D body. The smaller rectangle marks the USB end (approximate connector projection). Looking down at the top with board text upright, Pico is LEFT, USB points DOWN toward +Y. J1 is its RIGHT socket: physical Pico pins 20 through 1 from top to bottom. J2 is its LEFT socket: physical Pico pins 21 through 40 from top to bottom. J2 local pad 1 is Pico physical 21, not physical 1. The official USB-up component view is rotated 180 degrees, never mirrored.

The DUT sockets also mount on TOP. In that same top view the DUT is RIGHT: its left column J4 runs local pins 1–13 / ports 14–26 downward; its right column J3 runs local pins 1–13 / ports 1–13 downward.
