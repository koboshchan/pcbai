This experiment classifies isolated spoken digits zero through nine with 64 inputs, 16 rectified hidden neurons and 10 outputs. It uses Google Speech Commands v0.02 under CC BY 4.0, credited to Pete Warden / Google. The official compressed archive SHA-256 and official split-list hashes are recorded in dataset_provenance.json. Only digit WAVs and split metadata are retained locally. No dataset is committed.

Run python3 training/spoken_digits/prepare.py followed by python3 training/spoken_digits/train.py on KAT-BOX only. For the new layouts, pass --layouts 4x16 4x16-alt to both scripts. Dependencies are Python 3.12, NumPy, SciPy, PyTorch with CUDA, Pillow and PyArrow (the latter two are imported by the existing fixed-model definition loader). The download needs enough room for a streamed 2.3GB archive and about 1.3GB of retained digit audio. No archive is stored. An initial extraction bug skipped ./-prefixed metadata; the corrected extraction was rerun and the archive checksum matched the official value.

Each 16kHz mono clip is trimmed or right-zero-padded to one second. Features use a 512-point STFT, 400-sample Hann window and 160-sample hop, power spectra, triangular mel bands spanning 20Hz to 8kHz and log10 power floored at 1e-10. Frames are partitioned into eight or four consecutive bins and averaged, then flattened band-major. This gives either 8 bands × 8 time slices or 16 bands × 4 slices. Per-feature 1st/99th percentiles are fitted on training clips only; values are clipped to 0..1, rounded to 63 duty steps and converted to 0..5V. Exact scaling constants are saved in preprocess_*.json. Official splits give 31,158 training clips, 3,643 validation clips and 4,107 test clips. Speaker IDs are disjoint across these splits.

All float baselines use Linear(64,16), ReLU and Linear(16,10), Adam at 0.0015 for 60 epochs, seed 42064 and validation-only checkpoint selection. Each hardware model starts from its corresponding float checkpoint and receives another 60 epochs. It reuses the existing fixed_model/train.py Net definition verbatim, with differential E24 resistor conductances, E96 balance resistors, shared source-loading KCL, active rectifier/clipping, QAT from zero-based epoch 15 and tolerance training from epoch 25. This means the hardware model has more training epochs than the baseline; its higher accuracy is not evidence that quantization itself improves recognition. Neither architecture nor the existing MNIST board population was changed.

The unchanged fixed_model/cuda_mc.py solver evaluates 1,000 boards per layout, seed 51005, on all test clips. Weight/bias resistors and the shared 10k input source resistors vary ±5%; feedback, balance, rectifier and ADC divider resistors vary ±1%. The fixed 50-ohm driver, 1-ohm follower, 13.5V swing, 12-bit ADC and 6-bit inputs are retained. Nominal plus three random boards agree with the NumPy physical solver within 1e-9V for score/hidden values and 1e-12V for ADC values. Monte Carlo samples are resistor-tolerance simulations, not measured hardware or a certified worst case. They exclude PWM settling, microphone noise, opamp offset and unrelated/non-digit speech.

8×8 still wins across all four layouts by hardware validation accuracy, at 69.12%. The 16×4 model reaches 63.00%, the requested narrow four-band model 50.34%, and the wider-spaced four-band alternative 65.08%. On the untouched official test split, 8×8 achieves 60.90% float accuracy and 64.09% nominal hardware accuracy. Its 1,000-board tolerance mean is 63.28%, minimum 61.63% and fifth percentile 62.48%. The 16×4 alternative achieves 53.57% float, 58.70% nominal hardware, 57.67% tolerance mean and 54.35% minimum.

For the selected 8×8 circuit, the largest directed confusions are eight mistaken for three (82 clips), nine for one (65), seven for two (61) and one for nine (56). Six has the strongest recall at 80.46%; five is weakest at 56.40%. This is a working proof of concept, not dependable general speech recognition. More training or better time alignment is a reasonable next experiment, but was not part of this fixed-budget comparison.

Each layout directory contains the complete metrics/confusion matrices, nominal resistor circuit and selected float/hardware checkpoints plus exported float weights. data/ is ignored. summary.json records the validation-selected layout. The generated circuit JSONs are experimental replacement resistor populations only; existing paid-board exports remain untouched.

The new 4×16 front end uses causal Butterworth bandpass filters centered at 1600, 2000, 2500 and 3150Hz. Each filter has two second-order sections (fourth-order bandpass), with third-octave -3dB edges at center / 2^(1/6) and center × 2^(1/6). Filters start from zero state per clip. Mean squared amplitude is measured in sixteen nonoverlapping 1000-sample windows, each 62.5ms, then log10-transformed with a 1e-10 power floor. This is log power, not a rectified analog-envelope circuit simulation. It is equivalent to twice log10 RMS before scaling, with the corresponding floor. Features are flattened band-major and use the same training-only percentile scaling and six-bit quantization as the earlier layouts.

The requested 1600/2000/2500/3150Hz model scores 44.56% float and 45.61% nominal hardware on test. Across 1,000 tolerance boards it averages 44.53%, with a 42.59% minimum and 43.75% fifth percentile. The 4×16-alt model changes only the centers to 500/1000/2000/4000Hz. It scores 57.63% float and 60.56% nominal hardware, with a 59.82% tolerance mean, 58.19% minimum and 58.92% fifth percentile. Neither beats the existing 8×8 model. Wider frequency coverage helps in this comparison; the requested upper-frequency cluster loses substantial accuracy under the same training budget.

Both new models received 60 float epochs plus 60 hardware epochs and 1,000 tolerance trials each. The existing 8×8 and 16×4 results were retained without retraining. Run check_results.py for all four delivered result sets, test_envelopes.py for synthetic filter/causality checks, and check_features.py on KAT-BOX with cached audio for split/quantization checks and exact feature reproduction from 60 sampled WAVs per new layout. All 38,908 sample names, labels and split assignments match the baseline. No physical PCB design or paid-board resistor export was changed.

For the narrow 4×16 hardware model, the largest directed confusions are five mistaken for nine (117 clips), nine for three (81) and one for two (66). For 4×16-alt they are eight for three (79), five for nine (68) and seven for two (65). These simulated filter/log-scaling front ends are not the existing populated analog input chain; the results do not establish the accuracy of that chain.

The aligned experiment adds 8x8-trimmed and 4x16-alt-trimmed. Run prepare.py and train.py with --layouts 8x8-trimmed 4x16-alt-trimmed. They keep the same official samples/splits, train-only scaling, seed, network, 60+60 epochs, validation checkpoint selection, quantization and 1,000-board Monte Carlo budget. Their controls are the already-delivered untrimmed 8x8 and 4x16-alt runs, not newly tuned baselines. The alignment settings were fixed before training; no test-driven threshold sweep was performed.

Endpointing uses DC-removed wideband power in 20ms windows at a 10ms hop. The threshold is the maximum of four times the clip's 20th-percentile frame energy, 4% of its peak frame energy and 1e-7 full-scale squared. Only runs of at least three consecutive above-threshold frames count. The interval spans the first through last qualifying run, retaining internal pauses, with 30ms margins and a minimum 80ms duration. If no run qualifies, the whole clip is retained. Silence, stationary noise and continuous high-energy speech therefore fall back safely to the original window rather than creating an empty clip. This is an energy endpoint detector, not a general speech/noise classifier; nonstationary noise can still fool it.

The retained interval is stretched in feature time, not by resampling the waveform. Uniform relative-time bins cover the entire selected interval with fractional-cell integration. The 8x8 variant pools the existing mel log-power frames, whose timestamps use the 10ms STFT hop; the 4x16 alternative pools the existing causal 500/1000/2000/4000Hz filter powers and then takes log10. Filters and STFT are computed on the original one-second audio, preserving pitch, original frequency bands and pre-onset filter history. Actual waveform resampling would shift digit spectra and test a different front end. The only intended change here is temporal alignment; STFT support still extends around frame centers, so mel endpoint precision is limited by its 25ms window.

An MCU can reproduce this preprocessing, but the existing analog board cannot detect endpoints by itself. Capture a one-second 16kHz mono buffer, needing 32kB at signed 16-bit resolution, and compute the same DC removal, 99 window energies, percentile, threshold and run/margin logic. After the endpoint is known, compute or replay the filterbank into normalized-time bins, apply the saved training scaling, and emit the same 64 six-bit PWM inputs. A buffered energy trigger with 30ms pre-roll and a silence hangover could detect the start/end in a live product, but it is not automatically equivalent to this fixed-one-second detector. Bit/feature parity and recognition accuracy must be tested for that live segmentation separately. Digital IIR filtering is plausible for the four-band variant; the mel/STFT variant requires an FFT-capable MCU. Analog envelopes, log conversion, microphone noise and actual MCU timing/precision are not validated by these resistor-tolerance simulations.
